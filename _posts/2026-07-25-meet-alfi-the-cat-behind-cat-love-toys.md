Before there was a website, a logo, or a single blog post, there was Alfi.

She was about as classic-looking as a tabby gets — brown and white, sharp stripes, and a pair of eyes that always looked like she was already three steps ahead of whatever you were about to do. She wasn't a rare breed, she didn't have a fancy pedigree, and she absolutely did not care. Alfi knew exactly who she was, and more importantly, exactly what she wanted.

This site — and the character you'll see popping up throughout it — is dedicated to her.

## She Wasn't the Boss. She Was the Owner.

Calling Alfi "friendly" needs an asterisk. She was warm, affectionate, and deeply loyal — on her schedule, her terms. If you didn't follow the script, she let you know about it, loudly and repeatedly, until the situation was corrected to her satisfaction. She had opinions about everything, and she was not shy about sharing them.

She was also, unmistakably, jealous. If she felt you'd been spending too much time with another human — not even another cat, a *human* — she made sure you knew she'd noticed. Other cats fared much worse: this was her territory, and she defended it without hesitation, chasing off intruders regardless of their size. More than one much bigger male cat learned to respect the boundary.

And yet, every single night, she'd find her way onto you — making biscuits on your arm, tucking her body against you, sometimes holding onto a finger with her paw like she was making sure you weren't going anywhere. She wasn't a lap cat. She hated being picked up or held — that was firmly against the rules. But closeness, on her own terms, was non-negotiable. She showed affection her way, and you learned to read it.

## The Foodie With a Superpower

Alfi liked variety. Plain food, day after day, was beneath her — she wanted things with a bit of sauce, a bit of flair. And she had one trick that never stopped being impressive: somehow, from an entirely different floor of the house, she could smell the exact moment a pack of chicken deli slices got opened in the fridge downstairs. Within seconds she'd come tearing down the stairs, announcing her arrival, absolutely certain her portion was overdue. To this day, nobody knows how she did it.

## Movie Nights, Her Way

Alfi had a show. If you were in bed and hadn't already put it on, she'd come find your phone and make it very clear that it was time — a particular video of cockroaches scurrying around that she'd watch with total focus, occasionally taking a swipe at the screen. It became such a fixture that we're planning a whole post just on cat-friendly videos and "cat TV," because apparently we're not the only household where the cat has a favorite show.

## The Best Toy Cost Nothing

For all the toys that came and went, her favorite was never one of them. It was the little fabric hanger tag cut off the collar of a t-shirt or top — the small loop that's usually just thrown away. She'd hook her claws into it like it was prey, drag it around, and go back to it again and again. It was, without question, the cheapest and most effective cat toy in the house — and a good reminder that the fanciest packaging doesn't always win.

(For what it's worth, we were always careful to put it away once playtime was over, since it's still fabric and not something to leave unsupervised — a habit worth keeping with any DIY toy.)

## Why She's the Face of This Site

Alfi wasn't a cuddly, easy, textbook-friendly cat, and she'd probably be annoyed at any attempt to make her sound like one. She was smart, demanding, fiercely loyal, occasionally terrifying to other cats, and completely, unmistakably herself. That's the spirit we want this whole site to carry — real cats, real personalities, real quirks, not stock-photo perfection.

She's not here to test toys anymore. But she's still very much the reason this whole thing exists, and she'll be watching over it — probably judging our toy picks — every step of the way.

*Rest easy, Alfi. Still best partners.*
